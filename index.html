02 / WHY WE’RE HERE

For the plant people
who are still learning.

Little Leaf Rescue started with a familiar moment: bringing home a plant because it looked lovely, then wondering what to do when it didn’t look so lovely anymore.

Our idea is to make plant care feel welcoming instead of intimidating. Small spaces deserve greenery, beginners deserve patience, and a struggling plant deserves a second look before it gets tossed.

Small steps. Strong roots. ✳

03 / NOTES FROM THE WINDOWSILL

A few things your plant
wishes you knew.

Three gentle reminders for anyone learning as they grow.

NOTE 01 · LIGHT

Find the light before you move the plant.

Notice where the sun lands during the day. Bright, indirect light is a useful starting point for many houseplants, but every plant is different.

NOTE 02 · WATER

Check the soil, not just the calendar.

Watering needs change with the plant, pot, light, and season. Check the soil instead of automatically watering every few days.

NOTE 03 · PATIENCE

One yellow leaf isn’t the whole story.

Look at the whole plant and its recent care. A single older leaf can be normal; repeated changes are a reason to investigate.

General beginner tips only. Plant needs vary by species and growing conditions.

04 / GOOD QUESTIONS

Before you
panic-water.

Quick answers for first-time plant parents.

Is Little Leaf Rescue a real business?

No. This is a fictional business created for a college web-design project. The services describe the proposed concept.

Do I need to know what kind of plant I have?

Not necessarily. A clear photo and details about its light, watering routine, and recent changes can help you start identifying it.

Does every plant need water once a week?

No. Watering depends on the plant type, soil, pot, light, temperature, and season. Checking the soil is more useful than following one schedule.

Can a plant with yellow leaves recover?

Sometimes. Yellow leaves can have several causes, so look at the whole plant and its conditions before deciding what to change.
